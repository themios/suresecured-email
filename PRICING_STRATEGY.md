# SalesWyze Pricing Strategy

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Prepared for:** owner / operator decision
**Scope:** how to price the reactivation service and the underlying platform, grounded in competitor research and our real cost structure.

---

## The one-paragraph answer

Your current offer (pay only when a job closes, roughly 10 percent, nothing up front) is the strongest acquisition hook in the market and it aligns your incentives with the client's. Keep it as the front door. But a pure revenue share is fragile for you in three ways: you do the work before you get paid, you depend on the client honestly reporting closes, and it does not scale past the hours your team can personally deliver. The fix is not to abandon it. It is to add two things beside it: a flat managed tier for clients who want predictability, and a self-serve platform tier that earns recurring money without burning your team's hours. Price against the benchmarks below, protect the revenue share with attribution and a short term, and you have a pricing model that both converts and compounds.

---

## What the market charges (researched)

Three different worlds sell versions of what you do. Their prices set the anchor in a buyer's head.

**Database reactivation services** (closest to your offer)
- Packaged plans run about $630 to $1,000 (UK vendors quote £497 to £797), usually project or quarterly.
- The economic story they sell: reactivating an existing lead costs $2 to $10, versus $150 to $400 to buy a new HVAC lead.
- The payoff they promise: 500 to 5,000 dormant contacts recovering $75,000 to $750,000 at a 5 percent reactivation rate on a $3,000 ticket. That is exactly your landing page math.

**Done-for-you cold email and outbound agencies** (the premium tier)
- $2,500 to $12,000 per month. Email-only runs $1,000 to $5,000. Add phone and it is $5,000 to $15,000.
- Pay-per-meeting pricing runs $150 to $600 per booked meeting.
- Named examples: OutreachBloom at $2,400 per month, Belkins starting at $5,000 and typically $7,000 to $12,000.
- They require 3 to 6 month commitments and 3 to 4 months of ramp. That is a lot of money and patience up front, which is where you win.

**Performance-based and revenue-share** (your model)
- Pay-per-lead: shared leads $20 to $60, exclusive $50 to $150 (roofing and remodeling $75 to $150, HVAC and plumbing $30 to $80).
- Revenue share, real reference point: Amazon Home Services takes 15 to 20 percent of the job (20 percent under $1,000, 15 percent over).
- The consistent finding: pay-per-lead misaligns incentives because the vendor gets paid whether or not the lead closes. Pay-on-close aligns them. You are already on the right side of this.

**Self-serve platform** (what your app could also be sold as)
- GoHighLevel, the obvious comparable, charges $97, $297, and $497 per month with unlimited contacts and users, plus usage on top (email $0.675 per 1,000 sends, SMS $0.0079 per segment, calls $0.014 per minute).
- Contractors already spend $150 to $400 per month stitching together CRM, email, SMS, phone, and scheduling. That is the budget you would be competing for.

## What it actually costs us to deliver

This is the part that makes the pricing decision, and the answer is unusual: our variable cost is close to zero. The expensive input is human time, not infrastructure.

| Cost | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Email sending | ~free to $0.68 per 1,000 | Gmail API is free up to ~500/day; a real ESP is ~$0.10 to $0.68 per 1,000. Negligible. |
| Email verification | ~$0.60 per 1,000 | MillionVerifier bulk, one time per list. A 10,000 list costs about $6 to clean. |
| AI (sequence writing, reply sorting) | a few cents per operation | OpenRouter. The AI already drafts sequences, which is what cuts your labor. |
| SMS (only if used) | ~$0.008 per segment | Plus 10DLC registration. Optional channel. |
| Voice (only if used) | ~$0.07 to $0.10 per minute | Retell. Optional channel. |
| Hosting | tens of dollars per month, fixed | Railway. Effectively $0 per additional client. |
| **Human delivery** | **the real cost** | Writing and tuning sequences, watching replies, onboarding. This is what limits how many clients one person can serve. |

**Unit economics, done-for-you:** a client who recovers $60,000 from their list pays you $6,000 to $9,000 at a 10 to 15 percent share. Against that, your hard costs are roughly $6 to clean the list, a few dollars of AI, near-zero email, and a handful of your team's hours. The margin is excellent when a job closes. The whole risk is whether it closes and whether you capture it.

**Unit economics, self-serve platform:** at $99 to $299 per month with under $5 of variable cost, gross margin is 95 percent plus. That is a normal, healthy SaaS shape, and it does not consume your team's hours the way the service does.

## The recommended methodology

Price by how the value is delivered, not by a single number. Three tiers, each aimed at a different buyer, all anchored to the research above.

### Tier 1 — Pay-on-close (keep as the flagship hook)

The "nothing to lose" offer that gets people to hand over a list. Keep it, but instrument it so it is safe for you.

- **Rate:** 10 to 20 percent of jobs that close from the reactivated list, on a sliding scale. Start new clients around 15 percent and improve toward 10 percent as monthly volume grows. This sits right inside the Amazon Home Services 15 to 20 percent reference, so it is defensible, and the sliding scale rewards the clients who send more.
- **Up front:** keep it at or near zero, because that is the entire magic of the pitch. Optionally take a small refundable audit deposit (say $250) that you credit back on the first closed job. It filters tire-kickers and covers the list cleaning without breaking the promise.
- **Protect it two ways.** First, attribution: replies and bookings must route through the platform so a close cannot happen invisibly, and the client agrees to report closes on the jobs you sourced. Second, a short term: a 90-day minimum engagement so you are not writing sequences for someone who vanishes in week two.
- **Why:** unbeatable conversion, aligned incentives, and it is the story your brand is built on. The only real weakness is collection, which the attribution and term address.

### Tier 2 — Managed subscription (for clients who want predictability)

Some businesses do not want a share of every job and would rather pay a flat number. Meet them there and undercut the agencies.

- **Rate:** roughly $1,500 to $3,000 per month, done-for-you, positioned as reactivation-focused. That undercuts the $2,500 to $12,000 agencies while sitting well above the $630 to $1,000 packaged reactivation vendors, because you deliver ongoing campaigns, not a one-time blast.
- **Optional kicker:** a smaller performance percentage on top (say 5 percent) so you still share in the upside without carrying all the risk.
- **Why:** predictable cash flow for you, a clean number for them, and it captures the buyer who would otherwise go to Belkins and pay triple.

### Tier 3 — Self-serve platform and seats (the SaaS, once billing exists)

This is how you grow past the ceiling of your team's hours. The multi-tenant app becomes a product other businesses run themselves.

- **Plans, benchmarked to GoHighLevel:** a Starter around $99 per month (a capped number of contacts, 1 to 2 seats, core sequences), a Pro around $249 to $299 (more contacts, more seats, AI writing, phone and SMS enabled), and an agency or multi-location tier higher up.
- **Metered add-ons on top of the plan:** list verification resold at a markup (buy at ~$0.60 per 1,000, charge $2 to $5), SMS and voice passed through with margin, and AI usage above a plan allowance. This is exactly where your earlier "bill seats for scrubbing" idea lives.
- **Why:** 95 percent margin, recurring revenue, and no linear labor. This is the tier that makes the business worth more than the sum of its clients.

## The percentage question, answered plainly

For the pay-on-close tier, **15 percent is the right starting number**, improving to 10 for high-volume clients. Reasons:

- It matches the only credible public benchmark (Amazon Home Services, 15 to 20 percent).
- Your variable cost is near zero, so the margin is in the labor, and 15 percent on a real recovered pipeline pays for the labor many times over.
- Starting at 10 leaves money on the table and makes the later "we can improve your rate" conversation impossible. Start at 15, earn down to 10. It is easier to lower a rate than to raise one.

Do not go below 10 on the done-for-you service. Below that, one client who does not report closes wipes out the margin on three who do.

## The risks to price around

1. **Attribution and collection is the hard problem, not delivery.** A revenue share only works if you can prove the job came from your follow-up and the client pays on it. Route every reply and booking through the platform, timestamp them, and put the reporting obligation in writing. This is a product feature as much as a contract term, and the app already tracks replies and opens, so you are most of the way there.
2. **Cash flow.** Revenue share pays late by nature. The managed subscription and the SaaS tier are what smooth it. Do not run the whole business on pay-on-close alone.
3. **Labor is the ceiling.** Done-for-you does not scale past your team's hours. The AI drafting sequences already helps. The self-serve tier is the real answer, which is why the billing work on the engineering backlog is also a revenue unlock, not just plumbing.
4. **Deliverability is a shared cost.** One client's dirty list sending spam hurts the sending reputation that every client shares if they send from common infrastructure. Price list cleaning in (or require it), because a blacklist event is far more expensive than a verification bill.

---

## What I would do first

1. **Keep Tier 1 exactly as pitched on the landing page**, but add the attribution mechanism and the 90-day term, and set the rate at 15 percent sliding to 10. This needs no new pricing page, just a cleaner agreement and the tracking you mostly have.
2. **Write a one-line managed option** ("prefer a flat monthly? ask about managed") for the buyers who flinch at revenue share. You will close some who would otherwise walk.
3. **Treat the SaaS tier as tied to the billing work already on the backlog.** When billing lands, launch Starter and Pro against the GoHighLevel anchor with verification and messaging metered on top. That is the tier that turns this from a service into a company.
